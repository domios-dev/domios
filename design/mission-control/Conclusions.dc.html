What the watcher would conclude, and to whom it would write Fifteen conclusions in priority order, then two readings. For each: the fields it reads, when it fires, when it is silenced, the sentence, and in the watcher version the recipient and the transitions on which it writes. Five fire on this plan tonight; the rest are silent because the team is healthy, which makes the near-empty card the common case.

When writing is warranted at all

A message is warranted only when it names an action the recipient can take and has not taken. Once when the conclusion becomes true, once when it stops being true. Never on the timer, never to confirm, never to a worker: a third voice in a worker's context is more of what is already wrong. To the Orchestrator for anything the Orchestrator can do. To the user only when the Orchestrator is the one who cannot: a held gate, a dead Planner, a silent Orchestrator, the account's budget. Conclusions that become true in the same wake go in one message. This is the lesson of the acknowledgement loop on this very plan, and the surface version needs none of it, because a sentence on a page cannot loop.

What degrades without the watcher

Thirteen of the fifteen conclusions are arithmetic. They compare timestamps, count rows, and read fields the daemon already computes. A tab is absent from the snapshot or it is not. A review has been unjudged for 197 minutes or it has not. Credit is one closure from the wall or it is not. None of it is inference, none of it degrades, and none of it needs a model. Running these on a timer would buy nothing except a second copy of the same arithmetic and a token bill for it.

Two conclusions are judgments over prose, and the watcher earns its place on exactly those and nowhere else. Reports that moved nothing fires on three or more reports against one task inside ten minutes with no transition and no new sha. What it measures is the shape of the reporting; what it stands in for is whether any of those reports said something. It is wrong in both directions. It misses the slow version: two empty reports eleven minutes apart are indistinguishable from two useful ones, so the loop that is actually expensive, an agent re-reporting its own unchanged conclusion every few minutes for an hour, sits just outside the window. It false-fires on fast real work: three genuine findings filed in quick succession on a task that correctly stays in progress look identical to three restatements of nothing, and that is the costlier error, because the sentence sends someone to interrupt an agent that is working well. A model reading the five reports resolves both. It is not doing anything clever; it is reading four hundred words and deciding whether they contain a claim.

The second is a recurring bounce reason across tasks. review_audit.reason is free text and the eight most common in history are eight unique strings; no threshold clusters them by meaning. A model reading three bounce notes and seeing they cite the same missing thing can, and the fix is the brief template, so the recipient is the Planner, who authors task bodies, not the Orchestrator. It is also the only conclusion on this canvas that nobody can confirm is correct, because the clustering has no ground truth.

And the watcher is not free. Its judgment is over text an agent wrote, so a confident report that says nothing can talk it out of the correct conclusion, a failure the count rule is immune to, because a rule cannot be persuaded. It wakes 288 times a day on haiku, reads the snapshot and bills for it, while the rules run when the page is opened and cost nothing when nobody is looking. Its memory is the plan's own stride log: each conclusion is written as a stride with a fingerprint and read back on the next wake, so once-on-true and once-on-false need no new storage. It is a Team cron, and it dies with the Team.

So the honest summary is a ratio, not a claim. Two conclusions out of fifteen improve, in the specific case where the question is about the meaning of text rather than the arithmetic of events. Everything else is identical in both versions, and in the version without the watcher it is identical and exact. That is why the surface version is the one to build, and why the two frames on this canvas differ only in the told-whom lines.
