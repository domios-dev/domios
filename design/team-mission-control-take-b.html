The menu, at every state

Five cards, five states, one item. This is the whole design.

One candidate was considered for a second item and cut: “Go to the agent's tab”. Clicking the card already opens the task, and the agent link belongs in the open task where there is room to name who and since when. A second item would turn a button into a list that has to be read.

Keyboard: the overflow opens on Enter, Space or Shift+F10 from the focused card, and Esc closes it and returns focus to the kebab. The confirm opens with focus on Keep it, never on the destructive button, so a held Enter cannot delete a task. The card itself stays a single activation target: clicking it opens the task, and the overflow does not steal that.

Part 5 · Options to choose between

Every option below is numbered. Point at one. The rail never gets denser in any of them — they differ in which single headline the cell chooses, and in what sits behind its door.

Converged, and not redrawn here

  • Board · Spec · Plan · Tasks, Board first and default.
  • Tasks are the rows; the agent is an attribute. No roster.
  • The plan is the masthead that owns the list, which is what makes “selected” structural.
  • The rail is three or four cells, each one headline, one small visual, one door.
  • Destructive verbs sit alone under a rule, and their confirm names what survives.
  • The phone ordering: plan head, then work by claim on you, then the rail as accordions.
