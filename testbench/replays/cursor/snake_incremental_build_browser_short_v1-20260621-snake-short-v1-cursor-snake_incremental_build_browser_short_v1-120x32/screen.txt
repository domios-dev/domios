For a canvas Snake game like yours, mobile controls need to work without a physical keyboard and without blocking
  too much of the playfield. These are the main approaches I'd weigh:
  1. Swipe gestures on the canvas — Swipe up/down/left/right to change direction. This is the most natural fit for
     Snake on phones: thumbs stay near the game, no extra UI, and it matches how many mobile Snake clones work. The
      tradeoff is accidental swipes (especially near edges) and no obvious affordance until the player tries it.
  2. On-screen D-pad (four directional buttons) — A small cross or four arrow buttons below or beside the canvas.
     Very clear and discoverable, good for casual players and kids. Downsides: uses screen space, can feel clumsy
     for fast play, and the thumb may cover part of the control area.
  3. Invisible touch zones (quadrants) — Tap the top/bottom/left/right half (or quarter) of the canvas to turn
     that way. Similar to swipes but simpler to implement and sometimes easier for quick taps. Can be confusing
     without a hint, and taps meant for pause/restart elsewhere don't apply here.
  4. Relative / drag-to-steer — Touch anywhere and drag slightly in the direction you want; the snake turns when
     the drag exceeds a small threshold. Works well one-handed and avoids fighting with UI layout. Less familiar
     than swipes or a D-pad, and needs tuning so small jitters don't change direction.
  5. Tap-to-turn (cycle or smart turn) — e.g. tap left/right side of the screen to turn relative to current
     heading. Compact but unintuitive for many players; I'd only consider it if screen space is extremely tight.
  6. Hybrid: swipes + optional D-pad — Swipes as default, with a small toggle or setting to show a D-pad for
     accessibility or preference. More code and UI, but covers both "I want it to feel like a phone game" and "I
     want visible buttons."
  7. Pause / restart on mobile — Separate from direction: a visible Pause button (since Space isn't available),
     and keeping your existing Restart button touch-friendly (larger tap target). Often paired with any of the
     above.
  What I'd lean toward: swipe on the canvas for direction, plus a Pause button and your existing Restart — minimal
  chrome, familiar on mobile, and fits a single-file game well. A D-pad is the fallback if you want maximum clarity
  over elegance.
  Which of these would you like — swipes only, on-screen D-pad, swipe + optional D-pad, or something else from the
  list?
