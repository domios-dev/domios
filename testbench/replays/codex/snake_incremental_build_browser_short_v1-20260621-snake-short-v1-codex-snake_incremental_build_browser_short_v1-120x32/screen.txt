• 1. On-screen D-pad
     Four directional buttons below the canvas. This is the clearest option for mobile and works well for Snake, though
     it takes extra vertical space.
  2. Swipe controls
     The player swipes on the canvas to change direction. This feels natural on phones and keeps the UI clean, but it
     can be less discoverable without a small hint.
  3. Invisible touch zones
     Divide the canvas or screen into directional regions. Tapping left/right/up/down areas changes direction. It is
     simple, but less obvious and easier to mis-tap.
  4. Hybrid swipe plus buttons
     Support swipe gestures and also show a compact D-pad. This is the most accessible and forgiving option, but adds
     more UI.
  5. Floating directional buttons over the board
     Put translucent arrow buttons near the canvas edges. This saves space below the board, but risks covering gameplay
     if not carefully placed.
